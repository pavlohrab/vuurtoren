## Explanation

Dutch spelling looks strange at first: _groot_ but _grote_, _bed_ but _bedden_. There is one rule behind almost all of it.

Cut a word into syllables (parts you can say on their own): _gro-te_, _bed-den_. A syllable is **open** when it ends in a vowel (_gro-_), and **closed** when it ends in a consonant (_groot_, _bed_).

- A **long** vowel (aa, ee, oo, uu) needs **two letters** in a closed syllable: _groot_, _maan_, _vuur_.
- In an open syllable **one letter** is enough, because it is long there anyway: _gro-te_, _ma-nen_, _vu-ren_.
- A **short** vowel (a, e, i, o, u) must stay in a closed syllable. So when you add a vowel ending, the consonant is **doubled** to keep the syllable closed: _bed → bed-den_, _wit → wit-te_, _zon → zon-nen_.

Two more habits:

- Dutch words never end in **v** or **z**. At the end they become **f** and **s**; when an ending is added, the v or z comes back: _brief → brieven_, _huis → huizen_, _lezen → ik lees_.
- **ie**, **oe**, **eu**, **ui**, **ei/ij** and **ou/au** are always written with two letters, open or closed: _lief → lieve_, _boek → boeken_.

### Pattern

- long vowel, closed syllable: `aa ee oo uu` → _maan, been, boom, muur_
- long vowel, open syllable: `a e o u` → _ma-nen, be-nen, bo-men, mu-ren_
- short vowel + ending: double the consonant → _bed-den, kat-ten_
- end of a word: `v → f`, `z → s` → _brief/brieven, huis/huizen_

## Examples

- Het huis is **groot**, maar de kamers zijn niet **groot**. | The house is big, but the rooms are not big.
- Ik heb twee **grote** tassen. | I have two big bags.
- In de tuin staan drie **bomen**. | There are three trees in the garden.
- De **katten** slapen op het bed. | The cats are sleeping on the bed.
- Wij **lezen** elke avond; ik **lees** een detective. | We read every evening; I am reading a detective story.
- Ik schrijf twee **brieven**. | I am writing two letters.

## Common mistakes

- Writing a double vowel in an open syllable: _grootte_ for "big" (that word means "size"). The adjective with -e is _grote_.
- Forgetting to double the consonant: _de katen_. A short a must stay closed: _de katten_.
- Keeping v or z at the end: _ik leez_, _de brieff_. Write _ik lees_, _de brief_.
