## Explanation

Dutch is the official language of the Netherlands, of Flanders (northern Belgium) and of Suriname. There is **one standard language** with **one spelling**. A Fleming and a Dutch person understand each other easily, but you will notice differences, a bit like British and American English.

### Pronunciation

In Flanders and the south of the Netherlands, the **g** and **ch** are soft. In the north they are harsh and scraping. Flemish speakers often pronounce the **w** closer to English "w", and the **ei/ij** and **ui** sound different.

### Address and pronouns

- In Flemish speech, **ge / gij** is the everyday "you" (with verb forms like _ge zijt, ge hebt_). In writing and formal speech, Flemings use _jij_ and _u_.
- **u** is used more widely in Flanders, also to parents and grandparents in some families.

### Words

Some everyday differences (Flanders → Netherlands):

- _goesting_ → _zin_ (desire, liking); _content_ → _blij_; _schoon_ → _mooi_ (in the north _schoon_ means "clean")
- _gsm_ → _mobiel_ (mobile phone); _frieten_ → _patat / friet_; _een pint(je)_ → _een biertje_
- _seffens_ → _straks / meteen_; _efkes_ → _even_; _allee_ (come on, well) and _amai_ (wow) are typically Flemish
- _nonkel_ → _oom_; _bomma, bompa_ → _oma, opa_
- Some words exist in both but mean different things: _plezant_ is Flemish for _leuk_.

### Grammar habits

- **Diminutives**: Flemish speech often uses **-ke** / **-eke** (_een manneke, een kindeke_) where standard Dutch uses -je.
- **Articles**: in Flemish speech, _een_ can become _ne / nen_ before masculine words (_ne goeie mens_).
- **Verb order** in clusters: Flemings more often put the finite verb first (_…dat hij het heeft gedaan_), Netherlanders more often last (_…dat hij het gedaan heeft_). Both are standard.
- **Let's**: Flemish _laat ons gaan_, Netherlands _laten we gaan_.
- In Flemish speech, question words may be followed by an extra _dat_: _Ik weet niet wie dat er komt._ Do not copy this in writing.

### What should you learn?

Learn the standard language. Then get used to the accent of the region where you live. Both varieties are correct.

### Pattern

- Flemish speech `ge/gij` = standard `jij`
- Flemish `-ke` = standard `-je`
- Flemish `laat ons` = Netherlands `laten we`
- cluster order: Flanders mostly `heeft gedaan`, Netherlands often `gedaan heeft`

## Examples

- Hebt ge **goesting** in een pintje? | Do you fancy a beer? (Flemish)
- Heb je **zin** in een biertje? | Do you fancy a beer? (Netherlands)
- Wat een **schoon** huis! | What a beautiful house! (Flemish)
- Ik bel je **seffens** terug. | I'll call you back in a moment. (Flemish)
- **Laat ons** beginnen. | Let's begin. (Flemish)
- Mijn **gsm** is leeg. | My mobile phone's battery is dead. (Flemish)

## Common mistakes

- Thinking one variety is "wrong". Both are standard Dutch; only some spoken forms are regional.
- Misunderstanding _schoon_: in the north it means "clean", in Flanders also "beautiful".
- Writing regional forms in formal texts: _Zijt ge…_ In writing use _Bent u…_ or _Ben jij…_.
