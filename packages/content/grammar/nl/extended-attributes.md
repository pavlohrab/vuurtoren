## Explanation

Look at these, from newspapers and formal texts:

- _de **door mijn oma gebakken** taart_ — the cake (that was) baked by my grandmother
- _het **in 1900 gebouwde** huis_ — the house built in 1900
- _een **voor kinderen geschikte** film_ — a film suitable for children
- _de **in Gent wonende** student_ — the student who lives in Ghent

English puts such information **after** the noun ("the cake baked by…"). Dutch, especially written Dutch, can put it **before** the noun, between the article and the noun. The last word before the noun is an adjective or a participle (past or present), with the usual -e. Everything that belongs to it comes in front of it, in the same order as in a subordinate clause.

**How to read it.** 1. See an article (_de, het, een, die_…) that is not followed by its noun. 2. Jump ahead to the noun. 3. Read the words in between as a relative clause: _het in 1900 gebouwde huis_ = _het huis dat in 1900 gebouwd is_.

**How to use it.** In speech, prefer the relative clause: _de taart die mijn oma heeft gebakken_. Use the extended form in formal writing when it makes a sentence shorter, and keep it short: two or three words before the participle are enough.

### Pattern

- `article + [extra info] + participle/adjective(+e) + noun`
- _de + gisteren door de burgemeester geopende + brug_
- = `article + noun + relative clause` → _de brug die gisteren door de burgemeester is geopend_

## Examples

- De **door haar zelf ontworpen** jurk was prachtig. | The dress she had designed herself was beautiful.
- Het **in de krant genoemde** bedrag klopt niet. | The amount mentioned in the newspaper is not correct.
- Dit is een **bij toeristen erg populaire** plek. | This is a place that is very popular with tourists.
- De **sinds maandag geldende** regels zijn strenger. | The rules that have been in force since Monday are stricter.
- Ze woont in een **door de gemeente gerenoveerd** huis. | She lives in a house renovated by the council.
- De **op het station wachtende** reizigers kregen koffie. | The travellers waiting at the station were given coffee.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting the -e on the last word: _de in 1900 gebouwd brug_. Brug is a de-word: _de in 1900 gebouwde brug_.
- Putting the extra words after the participle: _de gebouwde in 1900 brug_. They go before it: _de in 1900 gebouwde brug_.
- Overloading: _de door de in 2020 gekozen raad goedgekeurde plannen_. Split it up with a relative clause.
