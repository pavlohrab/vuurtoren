## Explanation

Demonstratives point at something: "this", "that", "these", "those". Choose by two questions.

1. **Is the noun a de-word or a plural, or a singular het-word?**
2. **Is it near (this) or further away (that)?**

The plural of a het-word is a de-word, so it takes _deze_ / _die_: _dit huis_, but _deze huizen_.

To introduce or point out something with **zijn**, use **dit** or **dat**, even for people and plurals: _Dit is mijn broer_, _Dat zijn mijn ouders_.

Without a noun, _die_ and _dat_ often replace a person or a thing that was just mentioned, at the start of the sentence: _Ken je Tom? Die woont hier om de hoek._ This is very common in speech.

An adjective after a demonstrative always takes -e: _dit mooie huis_.

### Pattern

- de-word or plural, near: `deze` → _deze stoel, deze stoelen_
- de-word or plural, far: `die` → _die stoel, die stoelen_
- singular het-word, near: `dit` → _dit boek_
- singular het-word, far: `dat` → _dat boek_
- introducing: `dit/dat + is/zijn` → _Dit zijn mijn kinderen._

## Examples

- **Deze** fiets is nieuw. | This bike is new.
- **Dit** huis is te klein voor ons. | This house is too small for us.
- Wie is **die** vrouw? | Who is that woman?
- **Dat** boek heb ik al gelezen. | I have already read that book.
- **Dit** zijn mijn ouders. | These are my parents.
- Ken je Sara? **Die** werkt bij de bank. | Do you know Sara? She works at the bank.

## Common mistakes

- _Dit stoel_. Stoel is a de-word: _deze stoel_.
- _Dit huizen_. Plurals take deze or die: _deze huizen_.
- _Deze zijn mijn kinderen_ when introducing. Say _Dit zijn mijn kinderen._
