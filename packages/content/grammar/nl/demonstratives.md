## Explanation

Demonstratives point at something: "this" and "that". Which one you use depends on the gender of the noun and on distance.

- With **de-words** and **all plurals**: **deze** (this, these) and **die** (that, those): _deze stoel_, _die mensen_.
- With **het-words** in the singular: **dit** (this) and **dat** (that): _dit huis_, _dat boek_.

The plural of a het-word is a de-word, so it takes _deze_ and _die_ too: _dit huis_ but _deze huizen_.

To introduce or identify something with _zijn_, always use _dit_ or _dat_, whatever the gender or number: _Dit is mijn broer_, _Dat zijn mijn ouders_.

An adjective after a demonstrative always takes -e: _dit mooie huis_, _die oude fiets_.

_Die_ and _dat_ also introduce relative clauses, and _dat_ is a conjunction as well (_Ik denk dat …_). The word order tells them apart: in those clauses the verb goes to the end.

## Examples

- **Deze** fiets is nieuw. | This bike is new.
- **Dit** huis is te klein. | This house is too small.
- Wie is **die** man? | Who is that man?
- **Dat** boek heb ik al gelezen. | I have already read that book.
- **Dit** zijn mijn ouders. | These are my parents.

## Common mistakes

- _Dit stoel_. _Stoel_ is a de-word, so it is _deze stoel_.
- _Dit huizen_. Plurals always take _deze_ or _die_: _deze huizen_.
- _Deze zijn mijn kinderen_ when you introduce them. Say _Dit zijn mijn kinderen_.
