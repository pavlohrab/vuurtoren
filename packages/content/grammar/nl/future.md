## Explanation

Dutch has no separate future tense. You have three options, from most to least common:

1. The **present tense** with a word that points to the future: _Ik ga morgen naar Brussel_. The time word makes the meaning clear.
2. **gaan + infinitive** for plans and for things that are about to happen: _Ik ga morgen koken_. It is also used when you can see something coming: _Het gaat regenen_.
3. **zullen + infinitive** for predictions, promises, offers and a somewhat formal tone: _Ik zal je helpen_, _Het zal wel goed komen_.

With _gaan_ and _zullen_ the infinitive goes to the end of the clause, exactly as with modal verbs. The forms of _zullen_ are _ik zal, jij zult/zal, hij zal, wij zullen_.

## Examples

- Ik **ga** morgen **koken**. | I am going to cook tomorrow.
- Het **gaat** vanavond **regenen**. | It is going to rain tonight.
- Wij **gaan** volgend jaar **verhuizen**. | We are going to move next year.
- Ik **zal** je morgen **bellen**. | I will call you tomorrow.
- Hij **zal** wel **komen**. | He will surely come.

## Common mistakes

- Using _willen_ for a plain future: _Ik wil morgen regenen_. _Willen_ means that you want something; use _gaan_ or the present.
- Putting the infinitive early: _Ik ga koken morgen_. It goes last: _Ik ga morgen koken_.
- Mixing up _gaan_ as a movement verb and as an auxiliary. _Ik ga naar huis_ (I am going home) has no infinitive.
