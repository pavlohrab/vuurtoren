## Explanation

Dutch has no separate future form like English "will". You have three choices:

1. **Present tense** + a time word. This is the most common: _Ik bel je morgen._
2. **gaan + infinitive**: plans and things that are about to happen: _We gaan volgend jaar verhuizen._ _Het gaat regenen._
3. **zullen + infinitive**: promises, offers, predictions and a more formal style: _Ik zal je helpen._ _Zal ik het raam openen?_ (Shall I…?)

With _gaan_ and _zullen_, the infinitive goes to the end, like with modal verbs.

_zullen_ also expresses that you **think** something is probably true, often with _wel_: _Hij zal wel moe zijn_ (he must be tired, I suppose).

For something that will be finished at a later moment, use _zullen + hebben/zijn + participle_ (see conditional perfect and the future perfect): _Om zes uur zal ik het af hebben._

### Pattern

- present + time word → _Ik kom morgen._
- `gaan` … infinitive → _Ik ga morgen koken._
- `zullen` … infinitive → _Ik zal het doen._
- forms of zullen: `ik zal, jij zult/zal, hij zal, wij zullen`

## Examples

- Ik **bel** je morgen. | I will call you tomorrow.
- We **gaan** dit weekend **schilderen**. | We are going to paint this weekend.
- Kijk, het **gaat regenen**. | Look, it is going to rain.
- Ik **zal** je morgen **helpen**. | I will help you tomorrow.
- **Zal** ik koffie **zetten**? | Shall I make coffee?
- Hij **zal** wel in de file **staan**. | He is probably stuck in traffic.

## Common mistakes

- _Ik wil morgen komen_ for "I will come tomorrow". Willen means "want". Say _Ik kom morgen_ or _Ik zal morgen komen_.
- The infinitive in the middle: _Ik ga koken morgen._ Put it last: _Ik ga morgen koken._
- _Ik zal gaan naar huis._ Simply: _Ik ga naar huis._
