## Explanation

Dutch has two kinds of infinitive after a verb. After **modal verbs** and a few others (_gaan_, _laten_, _zien_, _horen_) the bare infinitive follows: _Ik kan zwemmen_. After many other verbs the infinitive comes with **te**: _Ik probeer te zwemmen_.

Typical verbs with _te_: _proberen_ (try), _beginnen_ (begin), _besluiten_ (decide), _weigeren_ (refuse), _hoeven_ (need), _durven_ (dare), _schijnen_ (seem). The same pattern follows nouns and adjectives: _tijd om te lezen_, _zin hebben te komen_.

_Te_ stands directly before the infinitive, and the infinitive is at the end of the clause. If the infinitive is a separable verb, _te_ goes between particle and verb: _op te bellen_, _mee te nemen_.

The longer version with **om** (_om … te_) is common and sometimes required; see the next topic.

## Examples

- Ik probeer het huis **te** verkopen. | I am trying to sell the house.
- Zij begint een brief **te** schrijven. | She starts to write a letter.
- Hij weigert mij **te** helpen. | He refuses to help me.
- Het is leuk je **te** zien. | It is nice to see you.
- Ik ben vergeten hem op **te** bellen. | I forgot to call him.

## Common mistakes

- Using _te_ after a modal verb: _Ik kan te zwemmen_. Say _Ik kan zwemmen_.
- Leaving out _te_ after _proberen_ or _beginnen_: _Ik probeer zwemmen_.
- Putting _te_ before the particle: _te op bellen_. The order is _op te bellen_.
