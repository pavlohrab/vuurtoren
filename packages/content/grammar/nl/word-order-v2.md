## Explanation

Count the _elements_ of a main clause, not the words. An element can be one word (_ik_) or a whole group (_mijn oudere zus_, _na de les_). The conjugated verb always sits in **second position**, directly after the first element. This is the V2 rule.

The first element is usually the subject, but it does not have to be. Whatever you put first, the verb follows it. Everything else comes after the verb. Non-conjugated verbs (an infinitive or a participle) go to the very end of the clause.

So the verb stays put and the subject moves: with a different first element, the subject lands behind the verb (see the topic on inversion).

## Examples

- Ik **werk** vandaag thuis. | I am working at home today.
- Vandaag **werk** ik thuis. | Today I am working at home.
- Mijn zus **woont** in Gent. | My sister lives in Ghent.
- Na de les **drinken** wij koffie. | After class we drink coffee.
- Ik **heb** gisteren een boek **gelezen**. | I read a book yesterday.

## Common mistakes

- Putting the verb third after a time word: _Vandaag ik werk thuis_. The first element is _vandaag_, so the verb must follow it: _Vandaag werk ik thuis_.
- Leaving the participle or infinitive in the middle: _Ik heb gelezen een boek_. It belongs at the end: _Ik heb een boek gelezen_.
- Counting a long first element as several words. _Na de les_ is one element, so _Na de les drinken wij koffie_ is correct.
