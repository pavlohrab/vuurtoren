## Explanation

The most important rule of Dutch word order: in a main clause (a normal statement) the **conjugated verb** (the verb that changes with the person: _werk, werkt, werken_) is **always in second place**.

"Second place" means the second **element**, not the second word. One element can be several words: _Mijn oude buurman_ or _Volgend jaar in juni_ each count as one.

Usually the subject comes first. But you can start with something else, such as a time, a place or an object. The verb then stays in second place and the subject moves **after** the verb (see inversion).

Words like _en_, _maar_, _of_, _want_ and the word _ja_ or _nee_ do not count as an element.

### Pattern

- `[subject] [verb] [rest]` → _Ik drink koffie._
- `[time] [verb] [subject] [rest]` → _'s Ochtends drink ik koffie._
- `[object] [verb] [subject] [rest]` → _Koffie drink ik niet._

## Examples

- Ik **drink** elke ochtend koffie. | I drink coffee every morning.
- Elke ochtend **drink** ik koffie. | Every morning I drink coffee.
- Mijn oudste zus **werkt** in Amsterdam. | My oldest sister works in Amsterdam.
- In Amsterdam **werkt** mijn oudste zus. | My oldest sister works in Amsterdam.
- Morgen **gaan** we naar de markt. | Tomorrow we are going to the market.
- Ja, dat **weet** ik. | Yes, I know that.

## Common mistakes

- Keeping English order after a time word: _Morgen ik ga naar school._ The verb must be second: _Morgen ga ik naar school._
- Counting words instead of elements: _Mijn broer werkt_ is right; _Mijn werkt broer_ is not.
- Putting an adverb between subject and verb, as in English "I often eat": _Ik vaak eet._ Say _Ik eet vaak._
