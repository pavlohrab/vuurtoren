## Explanation

Many Dutch sentences have **two verb parts**. They do not stand together, as in English. They form a **bracket** (Dutch teachers call it the _tang_, "pliers"):

- the **conjugated verb** stays in **second place**;
- the **other verb part** goes to the **end** of the clause.

Everything else goes **inside** the bracket: objects, time, place and _niet_.

The end part can be:

- an **infinitive** after a modal, _gaan_ or _zullen_: _Ik **wil** morgen een fiets **kopen**._
- a **past participle** in the perfect: _Ik **heb** gisteren een fiets **gekocht**._
- the **particle** of a separable verb: _Ik **bel** je morgen **op**._
- with _te_ verbs: _Ik **probeer** vroeg **te slapen**._

Only a few things may come after the bracket, mostly a subordinate clause (_…, omdat…_) or a prepositional phrase, in speech and in writing.

### Pattern

- `[1st element] [verb 1] [middle part] [verb 2]`
- _Ik **moet** vandaag nog boodschappen **doen**._
- _Wij **hebben** de hele dag in de tuin **gewerkt**._
- _Zij **komt** morgen om tien uur **aan**._

## Examples

- Ik **ga** vanavond met mijn zus **koken**. | I am going to cook with my sister tonight.
- We **hebben** in Spanje veel paella **gegeten**. | We ate a lot of paella in Spain.
- **Kun** je me morgen even **helpen**? | Can you help me tomorrow?
- De trein **komt** om half vier **aan**. | The train arrives at half past three.
- Zij **heeft** haar sleutels weer niet **gevonden**. | She didn't find her keys again.
- Morgen **wil** ik niet zo vroeg **opstaan**. | Tomorrow I don't want to get up so early.

## Common mistakes

- Keeping the two verbs together: _Ik heb gekocht een fiets._ The participle goes last: _Ik heb een fiets gekocht._
- Putting time or place after the bracket: _Ik wil kopen morgen…_ Everything goes inside: _Ik wil morgen … kopen._
- Forgetting the particle: _Ik bel je morgen._ is fine for "call", but _Ik sta om zeven uur_ is not: _Ik sta om zeven uur op._
