## Explanation

An **object** is the person or thing the action happens to: in _Ik zie hem_, "him" is the object. Dutch has special object forms, again with a full (stressed) and a short form.

- **Things**: a de-word becomes **hem**, a het-word **het**, a plural **ze**: _Waar is mijn jas? Ik zie hem niet._ _Het boek? Ik heb het al._
- **ze** is the safe choice for "them" in speech, for people and things. In careful writing, people are **hen** after a verb or preposition and **hun** for "to them": _Ik zie hen_, _Ik geef hun een cadeau_. Many Dutch speakers mix these up; that is normal.
- After a preposition, use the full form for people: _met mij_, _voor jou_, _bij haar_. For things, Dutch uses _ermee_, _erop_ instead of _met het_ (see pronominal adverbs).

**Two objects.** With verbs like _geven_, _sturen_, _vertellen_, the person (indirect object) usually comes before the thing: _Ik geef mijn moeder bloemen._ If the thing is a short pronoun, it comes first: _Ik geef het haar_ (or _Ik geef het aan haar_).

### Pattern

- me: `mij` / `me`
- you: `jou` / `je`; polite `u`
- him / it (de-word): `hem` (_'m_)
- her: `haar` (_d'r_)
- it (het-word): `het` (_'t_)
- us: `ons`
- you (plural): `jullie` / `je`
- them: `hen` / `hun` (written), `ze` (spoken, and for things)

## Examples

- Ken je **hem**? | Do you know him?
- Ik bel **je** morgen. | I will call you tomorrow.
- Waar is de afstandsbediening? Ik kan **hem** niet vinden. | Where is the remote? I can't find it.
- Dat boek is goed; ik heb **het** twee keer gelezen. | That book is good; I have read it twice.
- Wil je met **mij** naar de film? | Would you like to go to the film with me?
- Ik heb de kinderen een verhaal verteld en **ze** vonden het leuk. | I told the children a story and they liked it.

## Common mistakes

- _Ik zie het_ for a de-word thing: _De bus? Ik zie het._ Use hem: _Ik zie hem._
- Subject form after a verb or preposition: _met ik_, _ik zie hij_. Say _met mij_, _ik zie hem_.
- _Ik denk aan het_ for things. Say _Ik denk eraan._
