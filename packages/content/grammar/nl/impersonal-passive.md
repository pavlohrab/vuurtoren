## Explanation

Look at these:

- _Er wordt gedanst._ — There is dancing. / People are dancing.
- _Er werd hard gewerkt._ — People worked hard.
- _Hier wordt niet gerookt._ — There is no smoking here.

Dutch can make a passive from verbs that have **no object at all** (_dansen, werken, lachen, roken_). There is then **no subject**. The sentence is about the **activity** itself. **er** fills the first position, like a placeholder. If another element comes first, _er_ may be left out: _In de keuken wordt gekookt._

The verb is always singular: _wordt_, _werd_.

It also works:

- with a **dat-clause** as the real subject: _Er wordt gezegd dat het morgen gaat sneeuwen._ (It is said that…)
- with **modals**: _Er moet harder gewerkt worden._ (People need to work harder.) _Er mag hier niet gefietst worden._
- in the **perfect**, with _zijn_: _Er is veel gelachen._ (There was a lot of laughter.)
- with a preposition object: _Er wordt over het plan gediscussieerd._ / _Er wordt naar je gevraagd._ (Someone is asking for you.)

Where English uses "people", "they" or "there is …-ing", Dutch often prefers this construction, especially in writing, on signs and in news. In speech, _ze_ or _je_ are alternatives: _Ze zeggen dat…_

### Pattern

- `Er + wordt/werd + … + participle` → _Er wordt gezongen._
- other element first: `[place/time] + wordt + participle` → _Op zondag wordt er niet gewerkt._
- modal: `Er + moet/mag/kan … + participle + worden`
- perfect: `Er + is … + participle` → _Er is hard gewerkt._

## Examples

- Op het feest **werd** tot diep in de nacht **gedanst**. | At the party there was dancing until late at night.
- **Er wordt** hier niet **gerookt**. | There is no smoking here.
- **Er wordt** gezegd dat de winkel gaat sluiten. | It is said that the shop is going to close.
- **Er moet** in dit team harder **gewerkt worden**. | People in this team need to work harder.
- **Er is** gisteren lang **vergaderd**. | There was a long meeting yesterday.
- Tijdens de film **werd er** veel **gelachen**. | There was a lot of laughter during the film.

## Common mistakes

- Adding a dummy subject: _Het wordt gedanst._ The placeholder is er: _Er wordt gedanst._
- A plural verb: _Er worden gedanst._ With no subject the verb is singular: _Er wordt gedanst._
- _Er is geworden gelachen._ The perfect has no geworden: _Er is gelachen._
