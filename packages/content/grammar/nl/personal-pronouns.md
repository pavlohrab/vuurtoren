## Explanation

Subject pronouns say **who does** the action. Most of them have two forms. The **full** form is used when you stress the word (to compare or contrast). The **short** form is normal in everyday speech and writing.

- Use **u** for strangers, older people, customers and in formal letters. Use **jij/je** for friends, family, children and colleagues of your age. In the plural, _jullie_ is informal and _u_ is formal.
- **ze** can mean "she" or "they". The context tells you which.
- **het** is "it" for het-words. A de-word thing is usually _hij_: _Waar is de sleutel? Hij ligt op tafel._
- **het** is also an empty subject, like English "it": _Het regent_, _Het is laat_.
- **men** means "people in general" (formal). In speech people say _je_ or _ze_: _Je weet maar nooit._

### Pattern

- I: `ik` (short in speech: _'k_)
- you (informal): `jij` / `je`
- you (polite, one or more): `u`
- he / she / it: `hij` / `zij`, `ze` / `het`
- we: `wij` / `we`
- you (plural, informal): `jullie`
- they: `zij` / `ze`

## Examples

- **Ik** woon in Utrecht. | I live in Utrecht.
- Kom **je** morgen ook? | Are you coming tomorrow too?
- Wilt **u** iets drinken? | Would you like something to drink?
- **Wij** gaan naar het strand, maar **zij** blijft thuis. | We are going to the beach, but she is staying at home.
- Waar is mijn fiets? **Hij** staat achter het huis. | Where is my bike? It is behind the house.
- **Het** regent al de hele dag. | It has been raining all day.

## Common mistakes

- Using _jij_ with a shop assistant or an older stranger. Start with _u_; they will tell you if _je_ is fine.
- Using _het_ for every thing. A de-word is _hij_: _De tafel? Hij is nieuw._
- Using the stressed form everywhere: _Wij gaan, en dan gaan wij eten._ Without contrast, _we_ sounds more natural.
