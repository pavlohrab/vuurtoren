## Explanation

The past perfect (_voltooid verleden tijd_) is the perfect with **had/hadden** or **was/waren** instead of _heb/ben_. The choice of _hebben_ or _zijn_ is the same as in the perfect.

Use it for:

- **Something that happened earlier than another past moment**: _Toen ik op het station aankwam, was de trein al vertrokken._ (First the train left, then I arrived.)
- **Regrets and wishes about the past**, often with _maar_: _Had ik dat maar geweten!_ (If only I had known that!)
- **Unreal past situations** ("if … had …"): _Als je het had gevraagd, had ik je geholpen._ Here the past perfect appears in both halves; the main clause can also use _zou hebben_ (see conditional perfect).

### Pattern

- `had/hadden` … participle → _Ik had al gegeten._
- `was/waren` … participle (zijn-verbs) → _Zij was al vertrokken._
- regret: `Had/Was … maar + participle!` → _Was ik maar thuisgebleven!_
- unreal past: `Als … had/was … participle, had/was … participle`

## Examples

- Ik **had** het boek al **gelezen** voordat ik de film zag. | I had already read the book before I saw the film.
- Toen we aankwamen, **was** het feest al **begonnen**. | When we arrived, the party had already started.
- Zij vertelde dat ze haar portemonnee **had verloren**. | She said that she had lost her wallet.
- **Had** ik maar beter **opgelet**! | If only I had paid more attention!
- Als je eerder **had gebeld**, **was** ik **gekomen**. | If you had called earlier, I would have come.
- We **waren** nog nooit in Lissabon **geweest**. | We had never been to Lisbon.

## Common mistakes

- Using the simple past for the earlier event: _Toen ik aankwam, vertrok de trein al._ This means it was leaving as you arrived. For "had left": _was de trein al vertrokken_.
- _Ik had naar huis gegaan._ Gaan takes zijn: _Ik was naar huis gegaan._
- Word order in the subordinate clause: _…dat ze had haar portemonnee verloren_. The verbs go to the end: _…dat ze haar portemonnee had verloren_.
