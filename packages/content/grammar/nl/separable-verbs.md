## Explanation

Many Dutch verbs consist of a small word, the particle, and a verb: _opbellen_ (to call), _aankomen_ (to arrive), _meenemen_ (to take along), _opstaan_ (to get up). The particle carries the stress and is usually a preposition or an adverb such as _op_, _aan_, _mee_, _uit_, _af_, _in_, _terug_.

In a main clause the conjugated verb goes to second position and the **particle stays behind at the end**: _Ik bel hem morgen op_. In a subordinate clause, in the infinitive and with modal verbs the verb is whole again: _dat ik hem morgen opbel_, _Ik wil hem opbellen_.

In the perfect tense the particle sits in front of _ge-_: _opgebeld_, _aangekomen_. With _te_ the word _te_ sits between particle and verb: _op te bellen_.

Be careful: not every verb with a prefix is separable. Verbs that start with _be-_, _ver-_, _ont-_, _her-_ or _ge-_ never separate (_bezoeken_, _vergeten_).

## Examples

- Hij **belt** zijn moeder **op**. | He calls his mother.
- Wij **komen** om vijf uur **aan**. | We arrive at five o'clock.
- Ik **sta** altijd vroeg **op**. | I always get up early.
- Zij heeft haar vriend **opgebeld**. | She phoned her boyfriend.
- Ik wil de kamer **opruimen**. | I want to tidy the room.

## Common mistakes

- Not splitting in a main clause: _Ik opbel hem morgen_. Split it: _Ik bel hem morgen op_.
- Splitting where the verb stays whole: _Ik wil hem op bellen_. Say _Ik wil hem opbellen_.
- Splitting a verb that is not separable: _Ik zoek je op_ is right for _opzoeken_, but _bezoeken_ never splits. Say _Ik bezoek je_.
