## Explanation

Some verbs are made of a **particle** + a verb: _op + bellen = opbellen_ (to call), _mee + nemen = meenemen_ (to take along). The particle is **stressed**: _OPbellen_. These verbs are **separable**.

- In a **main clause** in the present or simple past, the particle goes to the **end**: _Ik bel je morgen op._
- When the verb is at the end anyway, the parts stay **together**: after a modal (_Ik wil je opbellen_), in a subordinate clause (_…omdat ik je opbel_).
- In the **past participle**, _ge-_ goes **between** the parts: _opgebeld_, _meegenomen_, _aangekomen_.
- With **te**, te also goes between them, and you write three words: _op te bellen_.

Common particles: _aan, af, bij, door, in, mee, na, om, op, over, terug, toe, uit, voor, weg_, and words like _thuis_ (_thuiskomen_), _schoon_ (_schoonmaken_), _kennis_ (_kennismaken_).

Some particles can also be inseparable prefixes (_door, over, om, onder, voor_). Then the stress tells you: see inseparable prefixes.

### Pattern

- main clause: `[verb] … [particle]` → _Ik ruim mijn kamer op._
- verb at the end: one word → _Ik moet mijn kamer opruimen._ / _…dat ik mijn kamer opruim._
- participle: `particle + ge + verb` → _opgeruimd_
- with te: `particle + te + verb` → _op te ruimen_

## Examples

- Ik **sta** elke dag om zeven uur **op**. | I get up at seven every day.
- **Neem** je een paraplu **mee**? | Are you taking an umbrella with you?
- De trein **kwam** te laat **aan**. | The train arrived late.
- Ik wil je morgen **opbellen**. | I want to call you tomorrow.
- Hij heeft gisteren eindelijk zijn kamer **opgeruimd**. | He finally tidied his room yesterday.
- Vergeet niet het licht **uit te doen**. | Don't forget to turn off the light.

## Common mistakes

- Keeping the verb together in a main clause: _Ik opbel je morgen._ Say _Ik bel je morgen op._
- _Geopbeld_. Ge- goes in the middle: _opgebeld_.
- _Te opbellen_. Te goes in the middle too: _op te bellen_.
