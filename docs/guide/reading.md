# Reading

Add a Dutch text, and Vuurtoren plans every page of it around the words you know. Each page leaves
about three new words for you to learn, and you can tap any other word to see what it means.

## Add a text

Open **Read** and press **Add text**. You can paste a text, give the address of a web page (Vuurtoren
takes only the article, not the menus), or choose a PDF or a photo. A photo is read with text
recognition, and you check the words it wasn't sure about before the text is added. With your own
AI key there is also **Write a story**, a new text at your level about a topic you choose.

A textbook goes in **Courses → Add a course**, as a PDF, an EPUB, photos of the pages or a list of
web pages. Vuurtoren splits it into units, and you read and practise it unit by unit.

**Read** also has 35 stories ready to open, from A1 to B2.

On a phone, a new text has to be prepared on your computer or your own server first, so it waits
until you sync. See [On your phone](../phones.md).

## What you see on a page

A page is about 150 words, and about three new words on it are marked to learn, with a soft colour and
a line under them. They are not translated,
so you can try to work them out from the sentence first. The number of new words follows how you
read: when you look up only a few words, the next pages get one more new word, and when you look up
a lot, they get one fewer. If you'd rather choose the number yourself, set **Words to learn per
page** in the reading settings (the **Aa** button above the page).

Tap a word to see what it means in this sentence, its dictionary form and the grammar around it.
**Add to my words** puts it in your reviews (**Add to Anki** if you work with Anki), and **I know
it** stops Vuurtoren from translating and teaching it.

## Four ways to read

You switch the reading mode at the top of the page. **Read** is the page as described above.
**Gloss** puts English above the words you may not know, so you can read on without stopping, and
the **Translations** slider in the reading settings sets how many words get it. **Cloze** turns some
words you know or are learning into blanks to fill in, and a right answer counts as practice for that
word. **Side by side** shows each sentence with its English, and the same words have the same colour
on both sides.

**Focus mode**, in the menu above the page (or the F key), hides everything except the text.

## Grammar and sound

Grammar like inversion or a separable verb is marked in the text and explained in the word card.
Every mark links to its topic in **Grammar**, where there are 63 topics from A1 to C1.

The common words, their example sentences and the stories are recorded in a female and a male
voice, and you can pick one in Settings → Reading → **Voice**. For other words the app uses the
Dutch voice of your computer or phone. Most Linux computers don't have one, so there Vuurtoren
uses its own Dutch voice, which it downloads with the rest of the language data when you start it
the first time.

On a phone you download the recordings in the same place in Settings. It's about 230 MB, and the
app starts the download by itself on the first start if you're on Wi-Fi. Until it's there, the
phone's own voice reads everything. If you want to know which voice you hear, press **Test the
voice**. If the speaker is crossed out on your phone, the phone has no Dutch voice, and you can add
one in its speech settings.
